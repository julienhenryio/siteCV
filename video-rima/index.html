Instagram Reel · three creative directions

Sugar cravings: one voice, three looks

Adisty, a dietitian, recorded a voice note in Indonesian about why we crave sugar. From that single recording we made three complete 45-second Reels with English subtitles. Same words, same story, same timing; each one has its own colours, type, motion and music. Watch them, pick a favourite, and read below what each one was trying to do.

What all three share

The story

A client's quote ("I think I crave sugar as a quick source of pleasure") → the myth busted: it's not about self-control → three triggers: the brain, the body, the environment → the real solution: find out what your body needs → mindful eating.

The voice

Adisty's own voice, cut from 1 min 37 to 43 seconds without changing her advice, cleaned of background noise and levelled for Instagram. Nothing was re-recorded or added.

The subtitles

Translated from Indonesian into short, natural English, one or two lines at a time, timed to her words. Key words are highlighted in the accent colour.

The format

Vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 44.9 s. All text sits inside the zone Instagram leaves free (away from the top bar, the caption and the buttons on the right).

Direction 1

Vintage Scrapbook

The most faithful reading of the brief: every idea gets its own handmade scrapbook page.

The idea

A scrapbook is how people keep things they care about, which is the tone Adisty uses: caring, not preachy. Each scene is a new sheet of paper (floral wallpaper, gingham, stripes, dots, graph paper) and the cuts are hard, like flipping pages. Food appears as photo cutouts with a white sticker border, as if clipped from an old magazine, and Adisty's own photo is the star of the collage: in an oval vintage frame when she busts the myth, as a cutout next to the coffee and the alarm clock when she talks about stress.

Artistic direction

  • cream
  • pastel blue
  • dusty pink
  • muted green
  • rose (emphasis)
  • plum ink
  • Type: Instrument Serif in italic for titles, a delicate editorial serif; Outfit, a simple clean sans, for the subtitles so they stay easy to read.
  • Motion: stop-motion. Stickers are "placed by hand" in three frames, and everything trembles slightly six times a second, like paper cutouts filmed frame by frame. Hard cuts between scenes.
  • Subtitles: small cream paper labels, slightly tilted, held by a strip of pink gingham washi tape.
  • Sound: music box, pizzicato strings and ukulele (100 bpm). A soft paper slide on each new section, small pops when stickers land, a rubber-stamp thud on "MYTH".

Easy to change

  • Replace any sticker or background (new picture, same place)~10 s · ~4 ¢
  • Use the second music take, or generate a new one5 s · or 1 min, ~8 ¢
  • Change a subtitle's wording1 min
  • Calm the jitter down, or switch to soft floating motionone setting
  • Change, move or mute any sound effectone line

Be careful about

  • The jitter is on purpose. It gives the handmade feel, but on a big screen some people may find it busy. Watch it once on a phone at full size before deciding.
  • The food photos are AI-generated and very realistic. Look closely once (the chocolate bar, the candies): if anything looks slightly wrong, it takes a few seconds to redraw it.
  • The rose colour was darkened so the highlighted words stay readable on cream. Don't make it lighter, or the contrast drops below what is comfortable to read.

Direction 2

Coloured-pencil Storybook

Adisty guides you through a children's book, one hand-drawn room per idea.

The idea

The reference video placed a real person inside illustrated rooms; this direction takes that idea furthest. Each trigger has its place: a bedroom at night for stress and tiredness, a kitchen for skipped meals, a sweet shop for the environment, and a garden picnic for mindful eating. The real photo of Adisty on drawn backgrounds is a deliberate contrast: she is the real person in the story world.

Artistic direction

  • warm cream
  • sage
  • peach
  • powder blue
  • butter yellow
  • terracotta (emphasis)
  • brown ink
  • Type: Caveat, a friendly handwriting, for titles and subtitles; EB Garamond in italic for the small lines, like a book's captions.
  • Motion: everything floats gently; scenes arrive with a soft page turn that slides over the previous one.
  • Subtitles: rounded cream bubbles with a pencil-coloured outline.
  • Sound: fingerpicked nylon guitar, celesta and a soft whistle (88 bpm). Lower, rounder pops and gentle page sounds.

Easy to change

  • Redraw any room or sticker (for example a different bedroom)~10 s · ~4 ¢
  • Faster or slower page turns, or plain cutsone setting
  • Switch the subtitles to a printed font if the handwriting feels too smallone setting
  • Use the second music take, or generate a new one5 s · or 1 min, ~8 ¢

Be careful about

  • Handwriting is the least readable of the three at small sizes. The subtitles are kept large (64 px) for that reason; check them on a phone in a bright room.
  • Page turns overlap the scenes by 0.4 s, so a scene's first word can appear while the page is still turning. It feels natural at normal speed, but shows if you pause on that frame.
  • Photo on drawings: if Adisty's photo ever feels "pasted on" rather than part of the page, a pencil outline around her can be added.

Direction 3

Sweet-shop Zine

The topic is sweets, so the video looks like a candy wrapper: bright, bold, made to stop the scroll.

The idea

The playful option. It keeps the scrapbook grammar (stickers, tape, frames, patterned papers) but prints it like a risograph zine: a few bold inks, visible grain and slightly offset colours. It turns the irony of the topic into the look: the video is as tempting as the sweets it warns about, while the message stays calm and kind.

Artistic direction

  • cream paper
  • cherry
  • bubblegum
  • cornflower
  • butter yellow
  • mint
  • navy ink
  • Type: Fraunces Black, a chunky soft serif, for titles; Nunito Black, round and very readable, for the subtitles.
  • Motion: bouncy pops with a little overshoot; stickers pulse gently on the music's beat.
  • Subtitles: white cards with a navy outline and a hard shadow; key words get a yellow highlighter stroke.
  • Sound: marimba, handclaps, finger snaps and toy piano (112 bpm). Brighter pops and quick whooshes on new sections.

Easy to change

  • Turn the beat pulse off or make it subtlerone setting
  • Change the highlighter colour, or the whole palettea few lines
  • Replace any sticker or background~10 s · ~4 ¢
  • Use the second music take, or generate a new one5 s · or 1 min, ~8 ¢

Be careful about

  • It is the furthest from the brief's "soft pastel, nostalgic" mood. It may suit a younger, fast-scrolling audience better than Adisty's usual followers.
  • The beat pulse makes everything move all the time. Fun at first sight, but if it distracts from what she says, turn it down.
  • The music has more bass than the other two. It was reduced in the mix so the voice stays clear, but on good headphones it is the punchiest of the three.

Before posting

  • Listen once on a phone. The sound was measured, not heard by a person: the music sits about 20 dB under the voice, the loudness matches what Instagram expects (−13.4 to −13.8 LUFS) and nothing clips. Only ears can confirm it feels right.
  • Read the subtitles against the Indonesian (below). The translation was reviewed, but Adisty knows best how she wants to sound in English.
  • Post the full-quality master, not the files on this page. These are lighter web copies (720 × 1280); the 1080 × 1920 masters are kept for Instagram.
  • Cover image: frame 0 already works as the thumbnail (title, her photo and the quote).

How these videos were made
  • Transcription: Whisper large-v3, run locally, then forced alignment (Meta MMS) to pin every word to within about 20 ms, so the cuts never clip a word. A voiceprint check confirmed a single speaker. The edited voice was transcribed again as a check: 98 % of words matched, the rest being spelling variants.
  • Translation: Claude Opus 5.5 via OpenRouter, phrase by phrase, keeping the original timings.
  • Pictures: stickers, frames and backgrounds generated with Google Nano Banana 2.1 (about 35 per direction), drawn on green and cut out automatically. Adisty's photos are the originals, with the background removed.
  • Music: generated with Google Lyria 3, two takes per direction, chosen by measurement (steadiness, bass, loudness). Sound effects: the HyperFrames library, each in two or three pitched versions so repeats don't sound copy-pasted.
  • Animation: HyperFrames (HTML rendered to video). The 13 scenes are shared; each direction is a "skin" of colours, fonts, motion and sound. Every sticker and sound is tied to a word Adisty says, so the edit can change without re-timing anything by hand.
  • Fonts: Instrument Serif, Outfit, Caveat, EB Garamond, Fraunces and Nunito (Google Fonts, Open Font License).
